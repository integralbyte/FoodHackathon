Sea salt in the recipe · published salt research

Our proposed broth uses sea salt. In a separate NaCl model-chicken-broth study containing L-tryptophan, salt reduced human-rated bitterness by about 30%. The study did not test Sorami or our sea salt. The dose and sensory result still need formulation trials.

Read the study ↗

MSG · savoury depth

Use MSG to build savoury depth alongside the grain and broth. Its dose belongs in recipe trials. No separate bitterness-reduction percentage is assigned to MSG.

Read the study ↗

Yeast extract · ≥40% less response from bitter sensors

At least 40% inhibition in 7 of 19 tested bitter receptors. This measures cells, not perceived taste. Published receptor result; not a Sorami taste test.

Read the study ↗

“Bitter sensors” means taste receptors tested in cells. A 40% smaller cell response does not establish 40% less bitterness when a person eats Sorami. The salt and yeast-extract findings come from different studies and cannot be added together.

Launch sequence

The presentation route is Cornell Campus in months 1–3, across Ithaca in months 4–12, and grocery stores after one year. These are proposed channels; no campus agreement or purchase order is claimed. The workbook’s underlying sales and cash scenario still assumes Cornell pop-ups and online packs; individual campus and Ithaca outlet costs have not been modelled separately. The workbook targets 6,000+ sales, repeat buyers and a taste-test win before expansion. Months 4–12: Upstate NY campuses and local grocers, remaining mostly direct. Year 2: broader retail after signed orders and a co-packer quote for 100,000+ cups per run.

The 6,840-cup plan is 4,400 full-price singles, 1,440 online pack cups and 1,000 introductory cups; card fees and discounts are included in the operating result. At the month-3 pace, remaining stock would take about 16 months to sell, beyond the planned 12-month shelf life. Begin regional sales by month 4 or reduce the initial batch. Retail at pilot cost loses about $0.15 per cup before overhead; scale is conditional on lower costs and orders. Shipping on online parcels is charged separately to buyers. Packaging design

The cost model proposes an insulated polypropylene (PP) cup, printed directly, with a high-barrier mono-PP peelable lid and no separate paper sleeve. This is a design target; the illustrated foil-lid package elsewhere in the pitch is concept artwork. The seasoning and toppings still use two high-barrier laminate sachets.

Campus pantry proposal

One cup donated for every 20 sold. At the modelled cup cost, this allocates about 9.5¢ per pilot sale or 3.8¢ per scale sale, before extra program delivery costs. The three-month plan would donate 342 cups. This is a proposed program, not an existing commitment.
