One preference at a time

Protein ≥10g → sodium <900mg → fibre ≥6g → not deep-fried. All three nutrition preferences apply per whole cup, including seasoning. The illustrated count is 21 → 11 → 8 → 5 → 1; using only confirmed competitor facts it is 21 → 11 → 8 → 5 → 5. These cutoffs are illustrative shopper preferences, not certifications of “high protein,” “low sodium,” or “high fibre.” In particular, under 900mg is not a low-sodium nutrient claim.

Standard cups leave at protein. Shin Black, Miyoka Chicken and Nissin Protein Chicken leave at sodium. NOMI’s three flavours leave at fibre. Nissin Soba Protein Classic and Chilli, plus immi Creamy Chicken and Roasted Pork Tonkotsu cups, leave last only in the illustrative scenario, which assumes they are deep-fried. Their actual frying methods remain unverified. All four have more protein and fibre than Sorami’s targets.

The basis behind the numbers

U.S. values use the complete-package manufacturer panels. Soba uses the UK retailer’s complete prepared serving: 210g Classic and 220g Chilli. Its 2.2g and 1.9g salt convert to 880mg and 760mg sodium (salt ÷ 2.5 × 1,000). Its stated preparation includes draining the water before adding sauce. Other cups include their broth; no sodium is subtracted by assumption.

The alternate table divides each whole-cup value by the listed dry package weight and multiplies by 100. For Soba this is prepared-serving nutrition per 100g of original package weight, not a laboratory dry-product analysis. Shelf filtering always uses whole-cup amounts.

What is a target?

Sorami’s 78g size, 11g protein and 400mg sodium come from concept packaging; 6.5g fibre is supplied by the team. Non-fried processing is a development target. Final formulation and manufacturing still need validation.

Sources checked 7 November 2026. Competitor pictures are manufacturer artwork; NOMI uses clearly labeled schematic illustrations. NOMI Golden Chicken and Tokyo Curry are listed as December 2026 pre-orders. Sorami is a concept; the shelves do not imply current retail co-location. Cup display sizes are illustrative.

A wish, not a health ranking

“I want the healthiest noodles” is the shopper’s wish. Meeting these selected preferences does not establish overall nutritional superiority. Protein quality, other nutrients, price, taste and health outcomes are not scored here.

The fourth filter is not deep-fried. The last four cross-outs are explicitly hypothetical: neither “wok-style” marketing nor palm oil in the ingredients proves a deep-frying process. The source data stays unknown, and Confirmed data mode does not exclude them. NOMI is manufacturer-confirmed air-dried and is excluded earlier only for fibre.
