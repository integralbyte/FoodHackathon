# Grain benefits

Updated 7 November 2026. Grain-ending callouts describe ingredient references, separately from the proposed finished-cup nutrition. The revised process specifies foxtail millet, so the old proso-millet nutrient figures are removed.

## Sorghum

[USDA SR28 cereal report](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400535/Data/SR/SR28/reports/sr28fg20.pdf#page=386), NDB 20067, gives 6.7g fibre, 165mg magnesium and 289mg phosphorus per 100g raw grain. Its fibre footnote specifies white sorghum. Values vary with variety and processing; they do not establish finished-cup nutrition. Per-100g captions remain absent from the visible presentation; the source basis stays in data and accessible labels.

## Foxtail millet

The displayed foxtail benefits use qualitative wording, avoiding a fabricated profile for the proposed flour:

- **Fibre-rich whole grain:** [Malavika et al., IJMR, Table III](https://ijmr.org.in/assessment-of-quality-of-minor-millets-available-in-the-south-indian-market-glycaemic-index-of-cooked-unpolished-little-foxtail-millet/) reports total dietary fibre in a studied uncooked, unpolished foxtail sample (*Setaria italica*). This is a whole-grain reference, not a specification for the flour in the proposal.
- **Magnesium + phosphorus:** [Japan MEXT food-composition record 01002](https://fooddb.mext.go.jp/details/details.pl?ITEM_NO=1_01002_7) covers raw milled/polished foxtail millet. It is a separate mineral reference and is not combined numerically with the unpolished-grain study.

The prior proso values (8.5g fibre, 114mg magnesium, 285mg phosphorus) and raw proso nutrient table are not used for foxtail. The whole-cup 6.5g fibre target remains a team goal.

## Other grain traits

[FAO, About the International Year of Millets](https://www.fao.org/millets-2023/about/) includes foxtail and sorghum in its millet discussion, including gluten-free grains and resilience in dry conditions. These are ingredient/crop traits, not certification of the final noodle product or its manufacturing environment.

Sorghum drought tolerance is also described by [UC Davis Sorghum](https://sorghum.ucdavis.edu/). Millet drought resistance is discussed by [FAO](https://www.fao.org/europe/news/detail/millet--the--forgotten-crop--is-making-a-comeback/en). No quantified Sorami water saving, disease-management claim or GI value is presented.
