# Grain benefits

Checked 7 November 2026. The ending presents ingredient benefits, separately from the team's proposed finished-cup nutrition. The displayed numbers describe raw grain, not a serving of cooked noodles.

## Fibre

[USDA National Nutrient Database, Standard Reference Release 28 (2015), cereal-grains report](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400535/Data/SR/SR28/reports/sr28fg20.pdf) reports values per 100g edible portion:

- **Sorghum grain, NDB 20067:** 6.7g total dietary fibre, PDF page 386. The record's fibre footnote specifies white sorghum; varieties and processing can change the amount. The page displays “Per 100g raw white sorghum.”
- **Millet, raw, NDB 20031:** 8.5g total dietary fibre, PDF page 130. The record identifies *Panicum miliaceum* (proso millet). The page displays “Per 100g raw proso millet”; this is not a value for every millet species.

The raw-grain figures do not establish Sorami's finished-cup fibre content or retention through processing. Protein is deliberately omitted from this ingredient scene so the presentation focuses on the selected strengths.

## Naturally gluten-free grains

[FAO, About the International Year of Millets 2023](https://www.fao.org/millets-2023/about/) includes sorghum and proso millet in its discussion of millets and states that millets are gluten-free. The callouts describe the grains themselves; they do not certify a finished noodle product or its manufacturing environment.

## Drought tolerance

- **Sorghum:** [UC Davis Sorghum](https://sorghum.ucdavis.edu/) describes sorghum as drought-tolerant and relatively low-input.
- **Millet:** [FAO, Millet: the “forgotten crop” is making a comeback](https://www.fao.org/europe/news/detail/millet--the--forgotten-crop--is-making-a-comeback/en) describes millet's drought resistance and suitability for limited water availability.

These are crop traits, not a quantified water-footprint comparison for Sorami noodles. The animation does not claim a percentage water saving or a measured environmental impact.
